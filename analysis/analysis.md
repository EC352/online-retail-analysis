## Revenue
The best month was September 2011 (£1,084,393.49, ~12% of total revenue), while the worst month was March 2010 (£23,558.61, ~0.26% of total revenue). 

A stark increase in revenue is seen from November 2010 (monthly growth of ~683%) continuing into January 2011 (264% growth), before revenue begins to fluctuate at a higher level. The dataset only runs until 09-12-2011, explaining the decrease in revenue in the final month. Examining the three largest orders in November (£5,375.28, £3,762.66 and £2,157.64) versus December (£8,520.92, £6,930.00 and £3,590.59) did not show one order driving the revenue increase in December. Although orders generated more revenue overall, the ten highest-revenue orders in November generated £19,059 in total compared with £36,063 in December, an increase of 89%. There was therefore no disproportionately large order in December driving the 683% revenue increase. Indeed, the largest order contributed only 3.78% of December's total revenue, compared with 16.32% in November. Furthermore, the average and median revenue per order remained relatively similar between November (average: £463; median: £228) and December (average: £409; median: £275). In contrast, the number of orders increased sharply from 71 in November to 549 in December, representing a 673% increase. This suggests that the December revenue increase coincided with a substantial increase in transaction volume rather than by a small number of unusually large orders.

Interestingly, the increase in transaction volume coincided with a substantial increase in the number of new customers. New customers increased from 40 in November to 272 in December, representing a 580% increase. New customer acquisition therefore increased on a similar scale to transaction volume, providing a plausible explanation for the sharp rise in orders, although the analysis does not establish a causal relationship. Moreover, new customers accounted for 56% of December orders and 58% of December revenue. Combined with the relatively stable average and median order values, this suggests that December's revenue growth primarily coincided with acquiring and converting a substantially larger customer base, rather than by existing customers placing significantly larger orders.

Following the December 2010 surge, revenue remained at a higher level throughout 2011, averaging approximately £632,957 per month. New customers continued to be acquired throughout every month of 2011, with an average of approximately 288 new customers per month. September 2011 represented the peak of this higher-revenue period, generating £1,084,393.49, or approximately 12% of total revenue. The average order in September was higher than in August (August: £430.71, September: £648.17), while the median remained similar (August: £302.40, September: £315.30), suggesting a few large orders may drive revenue in September. The largest order in September was £168,469.60 (while the second and third were £31,698.16 and £22,104.80 respectively), representing 15% of September total revenue. This order contained 80,995 units of Paper Craft, Little Birdie, generating £168,469.60 in revenue. September's record revenue therefore partly coincided with an exceptionally large order, meaning the month should not necessarily be treated as representative of underlying monthly demand.

Thus, the data indicates a structural shift in the business from late 2010 onwards: revenue growth initially coincided with a dramatic increase in transaction volume and new customer acquisition, after which the business maintained a higher monthly revenue level throughout 2011, culminating in its highest monthly revenue in September.

## Products
The highest-revenue product was PAPER CRAFT, LITTLE BIRDIE (£168,469.60, approximately 1.92% of total revenue). However, this revenue was generated from only one transaction (80,995 units sold), indicating that the product's high revenue coincided with an exceptional single purchase rather than consistent customer demand. In contrast, the REGENCY CAKESTAND 3 TIER generated £142,592.95 (1.63% of total revenue) and sold 12,412 units across 1,704 transactions, the WHITE HANGING HEART T-LIGHT HOLDER generated £100,448.15 (1.15%) and sold 36,725 units across 1,971 transactions, and the JUMBO BAG RED RETROSPOT generated £85,220.78 (0.97%) and sold 46,181 units across 1,600 transactions. These products therefore demonstrate broader and more consistent demand than PAPER CRAFT, LITTLE BIRDIE and can be considered the most popular products based on transaction frequency.

The top 10 products by revenue account for only approximately 10% of total revenue, indicating that overall revenue is not heavily dependent on a small number of products. This suggests that inventory planning should distinguish between high-value outlier products, such as PAPER CRAFT, LITTLE BIRDIE, and consistently high-demand products, such as the REGENCY CAKESTAND 3 TIER, WHITE HANGING HEART T-LIGHT HOLDER and JUMBO BAG RED RETROSPOT. The latter products may warrant greater inventory priority because stockouts could affect a much larger number of transactions.

## Geography
The United Kingdom was by far the largest revenue-generating market, generating £7,257,997.21 (82.88% of total revenue), with 4,256,451 units sold across 16,591 transactions (including PAPER CRAFT, LITTLE BIRDIE). The Netherlands was the second-highest revenue market, generating £283,748.49 (3.24%) from 200,737 units across 94 transactions, followed by EIRE with £262,114.56 (2.99%) from 140,487 units across 258 transactions. Germany and France recorded more transactions than the Netherlands and EIRE respectively (443 and 380), but generated lower revenue of £205,265.39 (2.34%) and £184,003.28 (2.10%).

Overall, these five countries account for 93.55% of total revenue, demonstrating a high level of geographic concentration. The United Kingdom alone accounts for almost 83% of revenue, making it by far the most important market. The relatively high number of units sold in the Netherlands and EIRE despite their low transaction counts also suggests that revenue in these markets may be influenced by larger orders rather than a high volume of transactions. Indeed, the average amount per transaction was £3,018.60 in the Netherlands, and £1,015.95 in the EIRE, while it was only £437.47 in the United Kingdom.

## Conclusion
**Revenue:** Overall, the analysis indicates that the business experienced a significant revenue shift in late 2010, and revenue growth primarily coincided with a substantial increase in transaction volume and new customer acquisition. Revenue subsequently remained at a much higher level throughout 2011, although the September peak was partly influenced by an exceptionally large order. Revenue is also highly concentrated geographically, with the United Kingdom accounting for approximately 83% of total revenue, highlighting both the importance of the domestic market and the potential for further international growth.

**Products:** From a product perspective, revenue is relatively diversified, with the top 10 products contributing only around 10% of total revenue. However, inventory management should distinguish between products generating revenue through exceptional individual orders and products demonstrating consistently high transaction volumes. Products such as the REGENCY CAKESTAND 3 TIER, WHITE HANGING HEART T-LIGHT HOLDER and JUMBO BAG RED RETROSPOT may therefore warrant particular attention when prioritising stock levels.

**Cancellations:** The analysis also identifies opportunities to reduce lost revenue from cancellations. Although cancellations represent a relatively small proportion of transactions, they account for approximately 5.42% of total revenue. Germany has a notably higher cancellation rate than the UK and France, while the REGENCY CAKESTAND 3 TIER and JAM MAKING SET WITH JARS are among the most frequently cancelled products. Further investigation into the causes of cancellations for these products and markets could therefore help reduce avoidable revenue loss.
